Conceptual Definitions

Concepts

1) External Soft Balancing

  • External soft balancing refers to an economic cooperation of a weaker state with outside actors to counterbalance the power of a strong neighbor. (Nurgaliyeva, 2016)

2) FDI Source Diversification

  • FDI source diversification refers to the extent to which foreign direct investment entering a country is distributed across different countries of origin, rather than concentrated among a limited number of source countries. (OECD, 2008)

3) Foreign-Policy Divergence

  • Erik Voeten (2013, p.13) defines the degree of congruence as the degree to which countries share common foreign policy interests and preferences.
  • Based on his definition, I define foreign-policy divergence as follows: Foreign-policy divergence refers to the degree of difference between two states' foreign-policy positions, as reflected in their observable behaviour and policy preferences.
Ladder of Abstraction

1. Ladder of Abstraction: External Soft Balancing

(The concept sits at the Low level)

High Abstraction: Balancing Behavior
  • Meaning: Any state strategy aimed at checking, offsetting, or reducing vulnerability to a stronger power (encompassing both military/hard balancing and non-military balancing).
Medium Abstraction - Soft Balancing
  • Meaning: + Non-military instruments only
Low Abstraction: External Soft Balancing
  • Meaning: soft balancing coordinated with other states

Quality: Strengths

  • Clearly distinguishes soft (economic/institutional) balancing from hard (military) balancing and specifies the asymmetric power context ("weaker state" vs. "strong neighbor").

Quality: Limitation

  • Broad enough that it cannot be captured by a single raw metric on its own; therefore, in my research design, it acts as the theoretical bridge connecting FDI diversification (the economic instrument) to foreign-policy divergence (the diplomatic outcome).
Ladder of Abstraction

2. Ladder of Abstraction: FDI Diversification

(The core Independent Variable sits at the Medium level, moving down to Low for measurement.)

High Abstraction (Broadest Scope): Economic Diversification
  • Meaning: Spreading a country's economic activity, trade, or capital inflows across multiple channels rather than relying on a single sector or partner.
Medium Abstraction: Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) Diversification
  • Meaning: Distributing cross-border direct investment across multiple dimensions (such as economic sectors, entry modes, or geographic origins).
Low Abstraction FDI Source Diversification
  • Meaning: The extent to which inward FDI entering a host country is distributed across different countries of origin rather than concentrated in a few source states (OECD, 2008), measured via the Inverted HHI

Strengths

  • The definition is clear, does not repeat itself, and explicitly excludes sectoral or other types of diversification, so the scope matches country-of-origin data.

Limitation

  • Captures formal legal origin of capital rather than ultimate beneficial ownership
Ladder of Abstraction

Ladder of Abstraction: Foreign-Policy Divergence

(The core Dependent Variable sits at the Medium level, moving down to Low for measurement)

High Abstraction (Broadest Scope): Policy Divergence
  • Meaning: Any difference in norms, stances, or policy behavior between sovereign states across international issue areas.
Medium Abstraction - Foreign-Policy Divergence
  • Meaning: The degree of difference between two states' foreign-policy positions, as reflected in their observable behavior and policy preferences (adapted from Voeten, 2013).
Low Abstraction (Operational Level): UNGA Roll-Call Voting Divergence
  • Meaning: The annual percentage of non-identical votes between two states in the UN General Assembly across four strict categories (Yes, No, Abstention, Non-voting).

Strengths

  • It focuses on real, visible actions (like actual votes) rather than guessing what politicians are thinking. Because we can see and count these actions, the definition can be tested with data.

Limitation

  • Countries show their foreign policy in many different ways (signed treaties or political speeches), not only through UN votes.
Measurement

Measurement: FDI Source Diversification (IV)

Note: Because external soft balancing is a macro-level theoretical mechanism rather than a third regression variable, it is operationalized jointly through the empirical covariance of your IV and DV

Indicator & Formula

  • Indicator: Quarterly distribution of gross inward FDI across source countries, measured using the inverse Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (HHI).
  • Here, sit represents the share of total inward FDI originating from country i in year t.
  • Scale: Continuous
FDI_Divt = 1 − ∑i=1N sit2

Justification & Variation

  • Why continuous? Categorizing years into "low" vs. "high" diversification would throw away fine-grained annual variation across a 21-year time series and prevent ARDL estimation.
  • Captures small changes: Using decimals (like 0.81 vs. 0.83) lets us see small yearly shifts in where investments come from.
  • Equal steps: The difference between numbers is always the same and consistent.
  • True zero: A score of 0.0 actually means zero diversification (all money comes from just one country).
  • Variation: Varies continuously between 0 (total concentration in one investor state) and 1 (equal distribution across infinite states). Empirically, Kazakhstan's score fluctuates over 2005-2025 as dominant shares held by the Netherlands, the US, and Russia shift alongside rising Chinese, Swiss, and Middle Eastern inflows.

Reliability & Validity

  • Reliability (Very high): The National Bank of Kazakhstan uses strict international accounting rules. Anyone who uses the same data and formula will get the exact same numbers.
  • Validity (High, with one minor catch): This index accurately measures where investments come from. However, some global companies route their money through low-tax countries (like the Netherlands or Cyprus) before investing in Kazakhstan. To make sure this does not skew the results, I will double-check my findings by running the test again without these countries included.
Measurement

2. Indicator: UNGA Non-Identical Voting Ratio (DV)

Indicator, Formula & Variation

  • Annual UN General Assembly (UNGA) Non-Identical Voting Ratio, sourced from the UN Digital Library System using strict four-category matching (Yes, No, Abstention, Non-voting).
FP_Divt = 100% − ( (Identical Votest / Total UNGA Resolutionst) × 100% )
  • Identical votes are resolutions on which Kazakhstan and Russia have the same voting category. Different categories are counted as non-identical votes.
  • 2. Variation: Varies continuously from 0% (Complete voting identity with Russia) to 100% (Complete voting divergence from Russia).
  • Across 2005-2025, variation is expected to reflect changes in Kazakhstan's voting alignment with Russia, including votes alongside Moscow (Yes/Yes or No/No) and instances of differing positions through abstentions or non-voting on resolutions concerning territorial integrity and human rights.

Scale Justification, Validity & Reliability

  • Individual resolution: Binary nominal classification (identical vote or non-identical vote).
  • Annual aggregate: Continuous ratio scale, expressed as a percentage from 0% to 100%, capturing degree of voting divergence over time rather than merely whether disagreement occurred at least once in a year.
  • Validity: UNGA voting records provide observable evidence of diplomatic alignment and disagreement. However, they capture only one dimension of foreign policy and do not necessarily represent the countries' positions in bilateral diplomacy, security cooperation or other international settings.
  • Reliability: Official UN Digital Library records and consistent four-category coding support replicability. Nevertheless, changes in the number and subject matter of resolutions across years may influence the annual ratio.
Research Design

Design Choice / Methods

Data Collection and Analysis Methods

  • Research Design: This study uses a single-country quantitative time-series design covering 2005-2025 (T = 84 quarterly observations). Using quarterly data substantially increases statistical power and captures rapid diplomatic reactions to geopolitical shocks.
  • Data Collection: Secondary macro-level numerical data will be collected from official institutional repositories (National Bank of Kazakhstan, UN Digital Library System, and Bureau of National Statistics of RK / IMF Direction of Trade Statistics) and compiled into a quarterly longitudinal dataset.
  • Data Analysis: The primary statistical estimator is an Autoregressive Distributed Lag (ARDL)
  • Justification: Unlike standard linear regression (OLS)-which produces misleading results when economic and political variables trend over time-ARDL accounts for time trends and works reliably even with smaller sample sizes; it also separates short-run annual diplomatic reactions from long-run structural alignment shifts.

Controls & Diagnostics

  • To prevent false correlations, the model includes two essential control variables:
  • (1) a Geopolitical Shock Dummy (2014 Crimea and 2022 Ukraine invasion) to absorb sudden shifts in the UN agenda, and
  • (2) Kazakhstan-Russia Bilateral Trade Share to separate trade dependence from investment diversification.
Sampling & Hypotheses

Case Selection, Temporal Sampling & Hypotheses

Case Selection & Temporal Sampling

  • Case Selection (Kazakhstan-Russia Dyad): Kazakhstan is a crucial/typical case of asymmetric interdependence in the post-Soviet space. Sharing a 7,600-kilometer border and deep security/economic ties with Russia (CSTO, EAEU), Kazakhstan simultaneously pursues a "multi-vector" foreign policy and attracts the largest volume of foreign direct investment in Central Asia. This makes it an ideal test case for external soft balancing-examining whether attracting diverse global capital gives a smaller neighbor enough leverage to distance itself diplomatically from a regional hegemon.
  • Temporal Sampling (2005Q1-2025Q4): Begins in 2005Q1 (earliest quarter with standardized, comparable NBK quarterly Gross Inward FDI by Country of Origin tables) and ends in 2025Q4 (most recent complete calendar year of finalized quarterly FDI and UNGA voting records), yielding 84 observations spanning a 9-year pre-crisis baseline (2005Q1-2013Q4) and major regional geopolitical shifts (2014Q1-2025Q4).

Link to Research Question and Hypotheses

  • Research Question: Can greater diversification of Foreign Direct Investment into Kazakhstan explain variation in Kazakhstan's foreign-policy divergence from Russia?
  • Alternative Hypothesis (H1): Greater diversification of inward FDI into Kazakhstan in prior quarters (t-k) is positively associated with Kazakhstan's UN General Assembly voting divergence from Russia in quarter t
  • Null Hypothesis (H0): There is no covariation between FDI source diversification and Kazakhstan's UNGA voting divergence from Russia (β1 = 0).
  • Link: Following deductive logic, the quarterly ARDL model tests whether empirical covariation exists between FDI diversification and foreign-policy divergence across 2005Q1-2025Q4. Lagging FDI diversification by one to four quarters ensures temporal precedence, testing whether shifts in capital origin systematically precede and co-vary with changes in UN voting behavior.
Sources & Constraints

Data / Evidence & Limitations

Data / Evidence

  • National Bank of Kazakhstan (NBK) Quarterly Balance of Payments Statistics: Provides quarterly Gross Inflow of Foreign Direct Investment into the Republic of Kazakhstan by Country of Origin (2005Q1-2025Q4) to calculate the quarterly Inverted HHI (IV).
  • 1. United Nations Digital Library System: Provides complete UNGA roll-call voting records with exact resolution dates (2005Q1-2025Q4) to calculate the quarterly Non-Identical Voting Ratio (DV).
  • 2. Bureau of National Statistics of RK / IMF Direction of Trade Statistics (DOTS): Provides monthly/quarterly Kazakhstan-Russia bilateral trade turnover as a share of total foreign trade (Control Variable).

Limitations

  • Covariation vs. Micro-Level Causal Mechanisms: Macro-level quantitative time-series design establishes systematic covariation between FDI diversification and UN voting divergence, but cannot directly observe behind-the-scenes diplomatic decision-making or prove policymakers' internal motivations (which would require elite interviews or archival process tracing).
  • Small Sample Size (T = 84): 2005-2025 yields 84 quarterly observations; limits how many control variables can be included simultaneously without reducing statistical power.
  • Offshore Financial Centers (Construct Validity Threat): Multinational firms frequently route investments into Kazakhstan through holding entities in the Netherlands or Cyprus, potentially slightly overstating true geographic diversification; mitigated via robustness check without offshore hubs.
  • Non-Binding Nature of UNGA Votes: UNGA resolutions are symbolic rather than legally binding, capturing public multilateral alignment while potentially overlooking quiet bilateral diplomacy outside the UN.
